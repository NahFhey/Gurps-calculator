# GURPS Alchemy: Official RAW vs Your System

Compared against GURPS Magic 4th Edition, Chapter 28 (pp. 210-222), GURPS Thaumatology (pp. 100-107), and GURPS Thaumatology: Alchemical Baroque (pp. 11-13).

---

## Official RAW Summary (GURPS Magic pp. 210-222)

### The Alchemy Skill
Alchemy is IQ/Very Hard with no default. It can be learned at half-speed without a teacher, but only in a mana area. Magery is not required and confers no benefit. Every elixir recipe is a Hard technique, most defaulting to Alchemy-1. Alchemical techniques may never exceed base Alchemy skill.

### Making an Elixir
1. Spend the listed material cost and begin brewing.
2. The elixir brews 24 hours a day and must be attended at least 8 hours daily.
3. Multiple alchemists may work the same elixir, but the lowest-skill alchemist makes the final roll.
4. An alchemist can only oversee one process at a time.
5. Multiple doses can be brewed simultaneously at -1 to the final roll per extra dose.
6. At the end of the required time, the alchemist makes a **single technique roll.**

### Critical Success and Failure
**"There are no critical successes in alchemy; either the process works or it doesn't."** This is a direct quote from the book — a key rule.

On a normal failure: ingredients are ruined, money wasted.

On a **critical failure**: make a second technique roll at -1 per dose in the batch. If that second roll succeeds, disaster is averted. If it fails, roll 3d6 on the Critical Failure Table:

| Roll | Result |
|------|--------|
| 3-5 | Everyone within 100 yards suffers the elixir's effect (or its reverse, 50/50) |
| 6-9 | Everyone within 10 yards suffers the effect or its reverse |
| 10-12 | Explosion destroys lab; alchemist has time to flee |
| 13-15 | Explosion destroys lab; alchemist takes 3d damage |
| 16-18 | Explosion destroys lab; alchemist takes 6d damage |

### Mana Effects
- Normal/High mana: works normally
- Low mana: twice as long to make, works for half as long (permanent effects still work)
- Very high mana: half the time to make, but ANY failure is treated as critical
- No-mana: elixirs cannot be made or used

### Laboratories
| Lab Level | Requirements | Skill Modifier |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Bare minimum (fire + containers) | Effectively nothing | -1 to skill |
| Basic ($1,000 equipment, table) | 100 sqft | +0 (avoids the -1 penalty) |
| Professional | 100 sqft, $5,000 equipment | +1 to skill |
| Cutting-edge | 200 sqft, $20,000 equipment | +TL/2 to skill |

### Forms of Elixir
| Form | Delivery | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| Potion | Drink | Affects one creature instantly. Loses power in a day if left open. |
| Powder | Eaten or dissolved | Takes 2d minutes. Lasts well; 50% chance per year of losing power if exposed to air. |
| Pastille | Smoke (lit tablet) | Fills 3-yard, 8-foot area. Affects in 2d seconds. Double time and cost to make. |
| Ointment | Touch/skin | Immediate effect. Lasts a week if exposed to air. Neutralized by water. |

### Alchemical Charms (p. 220)
Amulets (protective) and talismans (empowering) are made from elixirs. Time is 10x the elixir time. Same material cost. Hard technique, defaults to the elixir technique-2.

### Inventing New Elixirs (p. 212)
Uses standard GURPS invention rules with adjustments. Concept roll has a base -6 penalty plus twice the default penalty for the elixir. Making the first dose (Prototype) uses equipment modifiers: home lab -5, professional 0, cutting-edge +TL/2.

## Your System vs RAW: Detailed Comparison

### What Matches RAW

| Element | RAW | Your System | Match? |
|---------|-----|-------------|--------|
| Alchemy as IQ/VH skill | Yes | Yes | MATCH |
| 3d6 roll-under mechanic | Yes | Yes | MATCH |
| Lab quality affects skill | +0/+1/+TL/2 tiers | 0-4 rating scale | PARTIAL — see below |
| Four delivery forms | Potion, Powder, Pastille, Ointment | Potion, Salve/Poultice, Ink/Coating, Aerosol/Smoke, Bomb/Grenade | DIVERGED — yours adds tactical vectors |

### What Your System Changes

**1. Single Roll vs Work Blocks (MAJOR CHANGE)**

RAW: One single technique roll at the end of the entire brewing period determines success or failure.

Your system: Multiple "work block" rolls accumulating Progress Points (PP) toward a Work Requirement (WR). Each roll adds or subtracts from PP and Contamination Points (CP).

This is the biggest structural difference. RAW is pass/fail on one roll. Your system is a gradual process with granular outcomes.

**2. No Critical Successes (CONFLICT)**

RAW explicitly states: "There are no critical successes in alchemy; either the process works or it doesn't."

Your system: Critical success on 3-4 (or 5 at skill 15+, 6 at skill 16+) grants +2 PP and -1 CP.

This directly contradicts the book. If you want to stay closer to RAW, critical successes should be treated as normal successes. However, since your whole system is multi-roll, adding crits makes it more interesting — this is a reasonable homebrew choice.

**3. Critical Failure Effects (DIFFERENT)**

RAW: On critical failure, get a second chance roll. If that fails too, roll on a catastrophic table (explosions, lab destruction, 3d-6d damage, area effect).

Your system: Critical failure = +0 PP, +2 CP. No explosions unless the batch has the Volatile hazard tag. No second-chance roll.

RAW critical failures are much more dramatic and dangerous. Your system is gentler — a critical failure just adds contamination rather than potentially killing the alchemist.

**4. Lab Rating Scale (EXPANDED)**

RAW has three effective tiers: bare minimum (-1), basic/professional (+0/+1), and cutting-edge (+TL/2).

Your system: 0-4 scale that both reduces WR AND boosts effective skill on every roll. A lab rating of 4 gives a much larger bonus than anything in RAW, and it's applied twice (the double-dip issue noted previously).

**5. Contamination / Quality System (ENTIRELY NEW)**

RAW: Either the elixir works or it doesn't. No degraded outcomes.

Your system: CP accumulation produces a spectrum of outcomes (Clean, Minor Drawback, Unstable, Flawed, Mishap). This is entirely homebrew with no RAW basis.

**6. Elixir Forms vs Vectors (REIMAGINED)**

RAW forms: Potion, Powder, Pastille, Ointment — these are physical forms with different delivery mechanics.

Your vectors: Potion, Salve/Poultice, Ink/Coating, Aerosol/Smoke, Bomb/Grenade — with WR/DM modifiers.

RAW has no difficulty modifier per form (pastilles just take double time/cost). Your system adds tactical depth by making delivery method affect brewing difficulty.

**7. Reagent System (ENTIRELY NEW)**

RAW: Elixirs cost a flat dollar amount for materials. No individual reagent tracking, no aspects, no refinement, no potency levels.

Your system: Full reagent system with aspects (8 elements), refinement (crude/prepared/refined), potency (P0-P4), concentration steps, hazard tags, and ingredient roles.

RAW doesn't even name ingredients — the book just says "spend $X in materials."

**8. Tier System (ENTIRELY NEW)**

RAW: Elixirs have fixed costs and times. Difficulty is set by the technique default (most are Alchemy-1, some are Alchemy-2 through Alchemy-6).

Your system: Four power tiers based on potency load, each with different base WR, DM, and trait budgets.

RAW's equivalent to "tiers" is just the technique default: harder elixirs have bigger penalties (Resurrection defaults to Alchemy-6, Healing defaults to Alchemy-1).

**9. Hazard System (REIMAGINED)**

RAW: Hazards are handled purely by the critical failure table — explosions, area effects, damage. No ingredient-specific hazard tags.

Your system: Seven distinct hazard types with specific trigger conditions, WR/DM modifiers, and effects.

RAW is more dramatic (lab explosions), while your system is more granular (individual reagent properties causing specific complications).

**10. Multiple Doses (DIFFERENT MECHANIC)**

RAW: Brew multiple doses simultaneously at -1 per extra dose to the final roll. Same time, multiplied cost.

Your system: No explicit multi-dose penalty mechanic. Batch constraints are about reagent counts and units, not dose multiplication.

**11. Formularies and Technique Mastery (NOT IMPLEMENTED)**

RAW: Alchemists need a formulary (recipe book) to brew elixirs they haven't mastered. Without one, -6 penalty. Formularies are bought/found/stolen. Guild politics around secret formulas.

Your system: No formulary mechanic. Formulas are freely available in the tool.

**12. Mana Level Effects (NOT IMPLEMENTED)**

RAW: Mana level significantly affects alchemy (double time in low mana, any failure = critical in very high mana, impossible in no-mana).

Your system: No mana level modifier.

**Your system:** Lab rating reduces WR (needing fewer rolls) AND boosts each roll's effective skill.

**Effect:** Crude ingredients contribute 6 aspect points total (3+2+1), while refined contribute only 3.
**Question:** Is this intentional? It means crude reagents pump more points into the tally but with less control over which aspects. If the design intent is that refinement makes ingredients *better*, players might expect refined to contribute more, not less.

**Recommendation:** Consider capping PP gain per work block, or scaling the bonus differently. RAW doesn't have this problem because it's one roll = done.

## GURPS Thaumatology (pp. 100-107) — Optional Rules Analysis

Thaumatology is the official expansion book for GURPS magic systems. Its alchemy chapter (pp. 100-107) offers a range of optional rules that GMs can bolt onto the base GURPS Magic system. Several of these are directly relevant to your homebrew.

### Elemental Resonances / Special Modifiers (p. 101)

**What Thaumatology says:** The "Alchemy and Special Modifiers" sidebar suggests that materials associated with particular effects or types of magic — described as astrological or elemental "resonances" — could be required components for elixirs that produce similar effects. An especially appropriate, rare, optional material might give a bonus to Alchemy skill. It also discusses astrological timing influencing brewing (Astronomy rolls, day/month modifiers).

**Relevance to your system:** This is the closest official support for your aspect system. Thaumatology acknowledges that ingredients can have elemental affinities that matter mechanically. Your eight-element aspect tally (Water, Air, Fire, Earth, Vital, Mind, Shadow, Light) is a formalized version of what Thaumatology only sketches as a GM option. The difference is that Thaumatology leaves it vague ("the GM can decide"), while your system makes it a concrete mechanical framework with point tallies, dominant/secondary aspects, conflict pairs, and coherence checks.

**Verdict:** Your aspect system has official conceptual backing, even though its implementation is entirely your own.

### Laboratory Procedures (p. 102)

**What Thaumatology says:** Confirms the same three lab tiers from GURPS Magic: Home Lab (no modifier, $1,000), Professional-Grade Lab (+1, $5,000), Excellent Lab (+TL/2, $20,000). Also discusses "Analysis by Taste and Scent" — the idea that assessing ingredients can be hazardous (poisons, heavy metals, mercury fumes), which could justify advantages like Resistant to Poison or Danger Sense for alchemists.

**Relevance to your system:** The hazardous-ingredient analysis concept maps loosely to your Toxic, Intoxicant, and Hallucinogenic hazard tags. Thaumatology treats this as flavor/background, while your system makes it a concrete mechanic with trigger conditions and rolls.

### Alchemical Inventions — Complexity Table (p. 103)

**What Thaumatology says:** When inventing new elixirs, complexity is based on the elixir's default penalty:

| Default Penalty | Complexity |
|----------------|------------|
| 0 | Simple |
| -1 or -2 | Average |
| -3 to -5 | Complex |
| -6 or worse | Amazing |

The invention roll uses a base -6 penalty plus twice the default penalty for the elixir being invented. Prototype rolls use lab-based modifiers (-10 for no facilities, -5 for improvised, 0 for home lab, +1 for professional, +TL/2 for excellent).

**Relevance to your system:** This complexity scale is the closest official analog to your tier system. Both map elixir power to difficulty brackets. However, Thaumatology's complexity applies only to the invention process, not to routine brewing. Your tiers affect every brew (WR, DM, trait budget). The four-tier structure (Simple/Average/Complex/Amazing vs your Tier 1-4) is a near-parallel in concept.

**Verdict:** Your tier system extends an official concept from invention-only to all brewing — a reasonable expansion.

### Quick Gadgeteering and Alchemical Gizmos (pp. 103-104)

**What Thaumatology says:** Quick Gadgeteering lets an alchemist produce a preparation in minutes (equal to half the normal days). Alchemical Gizmos let alchemists pull one dose of any previously-made elixir from stock at market price. These make alchemists viable combat-support characters.

**Relevance to your system:** Your work block system already gives a time-based granularity that Quick Gadgeteering addresses differently. Where Thaumatology compresses days into minutes with heavy penalties, your system achieves variable completion time through the WR/PP mechanic. A skilled alchemist with a good lab can complete low-tier brews in 1-2 work blocks. The Alchemical Baroque book (see below) specifically warns against Quick Gadgeteering and Gizmos making practitioners too combat-oriented.

### Herb Lore (pp. 104-105)

**What Thaumatology says:** Herb Lore is a parallel skill to Alchemy — a "folk" approach using cheaper, more accessible ingredients. Herbalists can't use alchemical formularies; they use "herbals" instead. Material costs are 50% less. Herbalists can't make pastilles, can't analyze elixirs, and can't use the Gadgeteer advantage. Herb Lore has its own critical failure table (less explosive, more personal harm — sickness, equipment damage, deceptively normal-looking failed elixirs).

**Relevance to your system:** Your system doesn't distinguish between alchemy and herb lore. All ingredients go through the same aspect/refinement/potency pipeline. If you wanted to add Herb Lore support, you could: restrict herbalists to certain vectors (no Aerosol/Smoke or Bomb/Grenade), reduce material costs, and use a gentler critical failure outcome. The Herb Lore critical failure table (p. 105) is less catastrophic than the alchemy one — which actually matches your current gentler CP-based failure system better than the RAW alchemy table does.

### Consuming Multiple Elixirs and Binary Elixirs (p. 106)

**What Thaumatology says:** Multiple elixirs consumed together can interact dangerously — canceling, synergizing, or producing bizarre side effects. Binary elixirs are deliberately designed two-part preparations that only work when combined. Inventing a binary elixir uses the Alchemical Inventions rules at equal difficulty, with an additional -2 to skill and 25% longer production time.

**Relevance to your system:** Your system doesn't model multi-elixir interactions or binary preparations. These could be future expansion areas. Binary elixirs would fit naturally into your formula design — you'd just need two formulas that reference each other.

### Alchemical Cures (pp. 106-107)

**What Thaumatology says:** Targeted medical treatments using Diagnosis + Alchemy. Takes 3d minutes for diagnosis, then 1d+1 hours to prepare. Modifiers for lab quality, Gadgeteer advantage (+2), Quick Gadgeteer (+3). The "Kill or Cure" rule means any alchemical treatment causes 1d-2 points of injury (minimum 1) unless the patient makes an HT roll. Any Alchemy failure gives -1 to the patient's next HT roll, and critical failure inflicts 1d-1 injury (minimum 1).

**Relevance to your system:** Medical alchemy could be modeled as a specific formula category in your system. The injury-from-treatment mechanic maps conceptually to your contamination system — a brew with minor CP could represent a treatment that works but causes side effects.

### Elixir Delivery Methods (p. 107) — KEY SECTION

**What Thaumatology says:** Officially expands beyond the four base forms from GURPS Magic:

| Method | TL | Notes |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Smoking (cigar/cigarette) | TL4 | Like a pastille but single-person only, no extra time/cost |
| Pills | TL4 | Simple variant of powders, no extra time/cost |
| Injection | TL5 | Onset within 1d seconds |
| Aerosols (atomizers/pressurized cans) | TL5/TL6 | Can function like pastilles or apply refined ointments; double production cost |

The base forms (potion, powder, pastille, ointment) can also have onset times adjusted: potions 1dx5 minutes, powders 1dx5 minutes (or 2d seconds if snorted), pastilles 2d seconds to 1d minutes, ointments 1d minutes to an hour.

**Relevance to your system:** This is the most directly relevant section. Your five vectors partially overlap with Thaumatology's expanded delivery methods:

| Your Vector | Thaumatology Equivalent | Match? |
|-------------|------------------------|--------|
| Potion | Potion (base RAW) | MATCH |
| Salve/Poultice | Ointment (base RAW) | MATCH |
| Ink/Coating | No direct equivalent | YOUR INVENTION |
| Aerosol/Smoke | Smoking (TL4) + Aerosols (TL5+) + Pastille (base RAW) | PARTIAL — Thaumatology splits this into three methods |
| Bomb/Grenade | No direct equivalent | YOUR INVENTION |

**Verdict:** Thaumatology officially expands delivery methods beyond the base four, which validates the general direction of your vector system. Your Aerosol/Smoke vector combines what Thaumatology treats as separate methods (smoking, aerosols, pastilles). Your Ink/Coating and Bomb/Grenade vectors have no official counterpart — they're original tactical additions. The key difference is that Thaumatology's new methods don't add difficulty modifiers; they're just alternate physical forms. Your system ties each vector to WR/DM changes, which is entirely homebrew.

## Alchemical Baroque (pp. 11-13) — Setting-Specific Rules

Alchemical Baroque is a TL4 historical-fantasy setting book. Its alchemy rules are setting-specific but offer useful design perspective.

### Key Setting Constraints

Alchemy and herbalism operate at TL4. Alchemy skill gives no default to Chemistry. Herb Lore must be within two levels of Ritual Magic (Witchcraft). Alchemy cannot differ by more than two levels from Ritual Magic (Esoteric Philosophy), and it cannot exceed the average of Chemistry, Philosophy (Neoclassical), and Physics.

### Formularies in Baroque

Formularies are sold openly but quality is poor — no standard notation exists, and most alchemists are eccentric. Base cost is $200 per formula. Cheaper books exist at -10% to -70% price reduction, but each discount applies -1 to Alchemy when using that book's formulas. University libraries hold formularies but access is restricted to members.

**Relevance to your system:** This expands on the formulary concept that your system doesn't implement. If you ever add a formulary mechanic, the Baroque model of variable-quality recipe books with skill penalties for cheap ones adds interesting gameplay.

### "Other Optional Alchemy Rules" Sidebar (p. 12)

This sidebar explicitly discusses which Thaumatology options fit the Baroque setting:

**Recommended:** Alchemical Inventions (p. 103) for innovative research, Binary Elixirs (p. 106) for paired preparations, Ad Hoc Mixing (p. 106) for dangerous combinations, Alchemical Cures (pp. 106-107) for medical alchemy, and TL4 Elixir Delivery Methods (smoking and pills from p. 107).

**Cautioned against:** Quick Gadgeteering (p. 103) and Alchemical Gizmos (p. 104) — these make practitioners too aggressive and combat-oriented, which clashes with the setting's tone of scholarly lab work.

**Relevance to your system:** This is useful design guidance. Your work block system naturally avoids the Quick Gadgeteering problem because even fast brews take at least one work block. However, your Bomb/Grenade vector does push toward combat-oriented alchemy, which Baroque specifically warns against. Whether that's a problem depends on your campaign tone.

### Specific Elixir Restrictions (p. 13)

Some elixirs can only be made as ointments/unguents (Fire Resistance, Transformation, Flight, Invisibility). Regeneration and Youth elixirs are unreliable (HT+2 roll required, failure means the potion doesn't work, critical failure causes aging and 1d damage). Levitational Salts are a unique preparation type.

**Relevance to your system:** Your system doesn't restrict which vectors an elixir can use. Adding per-formula vector restrictions would add another layer of design depth if desired.

## Summary

Your system takes the GURPS 3d6 framework and builds a substantially more detailed crafting game on top of it. With Thaumatology in the picture, your system has more official backing than it initially appeared:

**Officially supported concepts:** Your aspect/elemental resonance system has conceptual backing from Thaumatology's "Special Modifiers" sidebar. Your expanded delivery vectors are partially validated by Thaumatology's additional delivery methods (smoking, pills, injection, aerosols). Your tier system parallels Thaumatology's invention complexity table.

**Still purely homebrew:** Work blocks with PP/CP accumulation, the contamination quality spectrum, the full reagent system (refinement, potency, concentration, roles, hazard tags), and WR/DM modifiers per vector have no official precedent.

**Conflicts with RAW that Thaumatology does not resolve:** Critical successes existing when RAW says they don't (no official source overrides this), critical failures being too mild compared to RAW's explosive consequences, and lab rating being applied twice.

**Gaps your system could fill from Thaumatology:** Formulary mechanics with quality tiers (from Baroque), mana level effects, multi-dose penalties, binary elixirs, ad hoc mixing consequences, alchemical cures for medical alchemy, and an optional Herb Lore parallel path.

**Design tension to watch:** The Alchemical Baroque sidebar explicitly warns against making alchemy too combat-oriented (cautioning against Quick Gadgeteering and Gizmos). Your Bomb/Grenade vector and fast low-tier completion push in that direction. Whether that's a feature or a problem depends entirely on your campaign's tone.
